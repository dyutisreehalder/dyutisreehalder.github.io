About

I study how the shape of a city — its buildings, its coastline, its sprawl — changes the climate that its people actually live in.

I am a climate scientist with over six years of academic research experience in GIS and remote sensing, focusing on how natural topography, urban morphology and human settlement patterns interact with climate processes. I integrate geospatial analytics, remote sensing, and climate science to generate spatial insights for climate-resilient and sustainable development.

My aim is to contribute through interdisciplinary research that bridges environmental science, urban systems, and policy development — using data-driven approaches to climate change in rapidly transforming landscapes, socioeconomic analysis, machine learning, and digital twin frameworks to support evidence-based planning and adaptation strategies.

I am currently working as an independent researcher exploring computational modelling of spatio-temporal datasets and AI-driven global and regional climate systems, including digital twin approaches. Alongside this, I have travelled to more than 20 countries, digging into global cultures, urban heritage, and everyday lifestyles to understand how socio-environmental dynamics shape communities and contribute to resilient, sustainable futures. I am motivated to explore how climate change will shape the future of cities and societies, and how responsible human behaviour, governance, and sustainable practice can drive meaningful, long-term solutions.
